CSV schema and analysis limits

Use exactly these five columns: representation,severity,incarcerated,guilty_plea,sentence_months. Representation: aid or private. Severity: lower or higher. Binary outcomes: 0 or 1. Sentence months: a non-negative number; use 0 when not incarcerated.

Only anonymized aggregate-compatible records belong here. Do not include names, case IDs, or contact information. Extra columns are rejected. Maximum 10,000 rows and 2 MB. The file is processed in this browser’s memory and cleared when you reload.

This prototype does not fit a double-machine-learning model, establish unconfoundedness, estimate causal effects, or predict individual outcomes. No confidence intervals are shown. Exported results are descriptive.

Fabio I. Martinenghi · Legal aid eligibility and court outcomes: a design-based double-machine-learning approach · supplied preprint, August 2026.

What the study asks

How does denying legal aid affect court outcomes for applicants charged with serious crimes in New South Wales? The study links aid applications from 2012–2021 to court records and uses double machine learning to account for observed assignment factors.

Why the nuance matters

Table 4’s ATT estimate for sentence length among those incarcerated is +5.791 months (95% CI: 2.628 to 8.954). Conditioning on incarceration selects a post-treatment group, so this is not a simple overall welfare comparison.

Identification depends on observed confounders and overlap. The estimates are not proof that declining aid improves any particular person’s outcome.

What this app adds

A practical preparation workflow for applicants, transparent descriptive analysis for researchers, and a capacity scenario tool for service teams. These are usable extensions of the paper’s question, not a claim of improved statistical performance.

Applicant answers never enter the research dataset. No accounts, external analytics, automatic storage, or backend are included in this local prototype.
