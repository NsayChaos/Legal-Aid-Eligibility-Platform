Collect once. Review together.

A guided intake captures the essentials and flags missing information before staff have to chase it. Keep phone, assisted, and in-person routes available.

Applicant→Shared intake→Staff review
Measure completion, repeat contacts, and minutes per intake.
02

Make the reasoning reviewable.

Surface eligibility questions, potential conflicts, and missing evidence for a qualified reviewer. Record the applicable policy version and the reason for every decision.

✓ Preparation assisted. Decisions human.
Measure corrections, review time, and missed urgent matters.
03

Share reviewed research across branches.

Reuse approved research and templates with jurisdiction, source, and review date attached. Route a matter with its context and confirm who owns the next step.

Local expertise↔Shared knowledge
Measure source coverage, rework, and referral completion.

How the calculation works

Annual hours = monthly accepted applications × 12 × minutes saved ÷ 60. Additional matters = annual hours ÷ assumed hours per matter, rounded down. Staff-time value = annual hours × hourly cost. This does not predict case outcomes, reduce the need for professional judgment, or demonstrate a causal benefit from software.

Quality belongs in the workflow

Keep professional review
in each workflow.

Efficiency is useful only when the service remains accurate, accessible, and accountable. Build these checks into the pilot.

[1]

Evidence you can inspect

Show sources, scope, and verification status alongside research. Require an advocate to confirm authority before use.

[2]

Decisions with an owner

Route uncertainty to a person. Keep approval, refusal, conflict clearance, and urgent case decisions with authorized staff.

[3]

Access with boundaries

Plan organization isolation, role permissions, consent-aware referrals, audit records, and retention rules before live data.

Where the product is today

This local prototype includes saved operational metrics, public-source snapshots and change reviews, policy drafts, and an activity log. Authentication, enforced organization permissions, AI retrieval, and case-system integrations remain future work.

About the paper that inspired Aid Atlas

Fabio I. Martinenghi’s supplied August 2026 preprint studies aid denial and criminal court outcomes in New South Wales using double machine learning. Its estimates depend on sample restrictions and identification assumptions. They are not U.S. eligibility rules, a reason to decline legal aid, or evidence that this prototype improves legal outcomes.

Your work stays local.

Landing demonstrations and the applicant/research tools process data in your browser. The operations workspace saves aggregate metrics, policy drafts, public-source snapshots, and activity logs to a local SQLite database. No accounts or analytics are configured. Downloaded files remain on your device.

Links to research sources open external websites with their own privacy practices. Do not enter confidential client information into demonstration interfaces. The proposed multi-branch security controls are not implemented here.
